## **Overview**

### Crafting Digital History in Academic and Public Spaces
**Theme:** What does it mean to 'Craft' Digital History? This week introduces the core concepts of digital history, digital public history and the foundational practice of creating linked notes.
 **Plugin of the Week:** **Templater & Hypothes.is** (Community Plugins).
	* **Optional Plugin: PDF ++** This plugin allows your to annotate PDFs within your Obsidian vault.
**Rationale:** This combination creates a seamless and powerful workflow between web-based social annotation (Hypothes.is) and private note-synthesis within Obsidian (via the Readwise plugin).

**Asynchronous Activities:** Read and annotate foundational DH articles using Hypothes.is.
*   **Plugin Application:**  Install PDF++ so you can download your assigned readings into your vault. 
*   **Assignment:** In your Obsidian vault, write a brief reflection on the concepts of "Past", "History"  and how our positionality influences the production of digital public history. Using backlinks, link your reflection to at least three other notes you created this week. Post a screenshot of your graph view.

*   **Asynchronous Activities:**
    *   Using a provided set of digitized sources, collaboratively annotate them with **Hypothes.is**.
    *   **Plugin Application:** Install and configure the Hypothes.is community plugin. Sync your Hypothes.is annotations into your vault. Process these imported highlights by creating new, more detailed notes.
*   **Assignment:** Write a short analysis of one primary source in Obsidian, making sure to link directly to the specific annotations imported via Hypothes.is.

This week, you will be introduced to fundamental concepts in the production of Digital History. First, I would like you to reflect on how we define the "Past" and "History," and how these definitions inform our approaches to Digital Public History? We will discuss how each author in the assigned readings defines the 'Past', 'History', Digital Public History, and Digital Humanities, and the implications of these definitions on crafting and presenting history on digital platforms to the public. This appears to be a lot of reading, but it will set you up for success in this course. Also, there is a sinister underlying reason for my madness: To teach you how to read scholarly work efficiently and effectively. See [How to read History in four easy steps]().  Each reading is 'seeded' with annotations by me; some of my annotations direct you to pay attention to particular issues or ideas. **Annotate** anything interesting you find with Hypothes.is while logged into our reading group, keeping in mind what you've already read. As a reminder, the invitation to join our reading group is [here](https://hypothes.is/groups/yzzrnv9q/hist-digh3814).
