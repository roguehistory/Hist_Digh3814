## Overview 
### Crafting Digital History in Academic and Public Spaces

This week, you will be introduced to fundamental concepts in the production of Digital History. First, I would like you to reflect on how we define the "Past" and "History," and how these definitions inform our approaches to Digital Public History? We will discuss how each author in the assigned readings defines the 'Past', 'History', Digital Public History, and Digital Humanities, and the implications of these definitions on crafting and presenting history on digital platforms to the public. This appears to be a lot of reading, but it will set you up for success in this course. Also, there is a sinister underlying reason for my madness: To teach you how to read scholarly work efficiently and effectively. See [How to read History in four easy steps]().  Each reading is 'seeded' with annotations by me; some of my annotations direct you to pay attention to particular issues or ideas. **Annotate** anything interesting you find with Hypothes.is while logged into our reading group, keeping in mind what you've already read. As a reminder, the invitation to join our reading group is [here](https://hypothes.is/groups/yzzrnv9q/hist-digh3814).
