## Module One: **How historians work/think in the digital age.**

![Nodes and Edges.png](/images/ObsidianImages/BandWNodes.jpeg)

This module is a method and theory-driven module that introduces students to the concepts and themes in academic history, public history, archaeology and digital humanities. We will explore the various digital methodologies and theories that are applied in their respective fields. Week one is all about familiarizing yourself with the syllabus and setting up your digital notebook. 

By the end of week two, you should have some familiarity with Obsidian.md and Hypothes.is (and for the bold, Zotero and GitHub). Your first graded assignment, the 'Tutorial Reflection, is due September 14th, 11:59 PM. See [Tutorial Reflection]() for instructions and rubric. Week three, we dig into the craft of digital history and archaeology (There may be a lot of archaeology puns/memes in this course). While we consider the history and progression of the disciplines, you will also need to think about a period from the ancient or medieval world that interests you. By the end of week three, you will have selected a period that interests you (e.g., 12th-13th century), a theme (e.g., the crusades), and a region (e.g. modern Greece), and you will then submit your proposed topics to [this document](). This will allow me to guide you on your chosen topic and keep you on track and focused. We all can go down rabbit holes. I do it all the time.

Week four, you will take what you are learning from the readings, digital tools you are exposed to, and begin exploring possible digital methods and theories you wish to apply to your research topic. We also navigate the rise of LLM and AI and consider if our world is doomed (a little hyperbolic, maybe, but...) and conclude with a case study of a digital 'tech' used to study the ancient and medieval world. 

For your consolidation week, you will "excavate" what we have learned from the previous weeks and consider the implications for your research subject. Is there a digital tool that interests you for your research? Could AI help with the study of the ancient world? What are the ethics you should consider when using digital tools to create "History"? By the end of Module One, students should have gained a general understanding of the development of history and archaeology under the auspices of digital humanities.
